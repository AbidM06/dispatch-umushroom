# I got IREN wrong. Here is what I learned.

*Paper-money experiment, no real money, not investment advice. Written 19 Oct 2026.*

On 12 Oct I bought IREN, the bitcoin miner turning itself into an AI data-centre company, as 4% of my GBP 100,000 test portfolio. On 19 Oct I sold all of it. It closed Friday at $26.99, against $36.31 at my entry: down 25.7% in five trading days. On the position that is a paper loss of roughly 1% of the whole portfolio, and the stock was the worst performer in the book by a wide margin.

I made the mistake before the price fell. My AI portfolio manager (Claude) told me on day one that IREN should be 3% at most until its big contract was confirmed. I kept 4% anyway, because I liked the story. The main piece of the story, a multiyear deal with Anthropic, rested on one report from anonymous sources, with no size, term or site. I had written that down myself as unverified, then sized the position as if it were true.

**Why it fell so hard**

1. **It is a leveraged bet on AI funding.** IREN had about $7.6bn of debt, raised $6.3bn of convertible bonds and $4.7bn of new shares in its last financial year, and lost $702.6m after $638.8m of write-downs. Some of its customers are financed at about 9%. That works while money for AI is cheap and plentiful.
2. **The money got nervous.** This week CoreWeave, the best-known company in the same business, fell 25%. Its bonds were yielding over 16% and the market was pricing roughly a 60% chance it defaults within five years (Bloomberg, via CNBC and others). CoreWeave dropped 11.9% on Friday alone. IREN fell 11.2% the same day, and Nebius fell 12.2%. I found no IREN-specific news; it traded with the group.
3. **A research note added fuel.** Gartner warned that over 80% of these "neocloud" providers may stop operating independently by 2030, and IREN touched $26.60 intraday on the day it was published.
4. **The chip sell-off landed on top.** The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 11.4% last week and the Nasdaq had its worst week since June.

None of this was a surprise in size. IREN's worst four-week fall in the past year was 43.9%. I had that number in front of me when I set the position size.

**What I learned**

- **An unconfirmed report is not a thesis.** If the case depends on a rumour, size the position as if the rumour is false.
- **Listen to the dissent.** The advice to cap IREN at 3% was the right one. Overruling it is allowed, but I should have written down what would prove me wrong before I bought.
- **My holdings were less diversified than they looked.** Nvidia, TSMC, Korea, the power stocks and IREN all depend on AI spending staying funded. When funding worries hit, they fell together.
- **Rules beat feelings.** I set a review level of $28 on day one. Friday's close broke it, and the decision to sell was easy because the line was already drawn. The same rule would have been much harder to follow if I had made it up on the way down.
- **Losses compound.** The portfolio is down about 5.5% against about 2% for the S&P 500. One oversized, story-driven position did a lot of that.

I am not saying IREN will not recover, or that the AI build-out is over. I am saying that I took too much risk on a story I could not verify, and the plan I wrote is what limited the damage.

The test runs to 7 Dec. I will keep posting the daily notes, mistakes included.
