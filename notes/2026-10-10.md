## What happened and why
- **Equities:** The S&P 500 gained 0.59% after Thursday's tech sell-off, when the Financial Times reported that OpenAI's annualised revenue is nearly $50bn, not the $70bn circulated. Part of the gap is accounting. TSMC fell 3.3% and Micron 4.3% that day.
- **Rates:** The Fed's September minutes showed most officials expect another hike by year-end. October hike odds are about 17% to 20% and December about 81% to 84%. The 10-year ended at 5.24%.
- **Commodities:** Oil eased on hopes of progress in US-Iran talks, though a hurricane shut in much of Gulf of Mexico output.
- **Company news:** TSMC reported record Q3 sales; Samsung guided to a record 107.4tn won operating profit, with sales below forecasts; Vistra's $4.2bn federal nuclear loan offer (5 Oct) continues to support the power names.

## What it means
The AI trade has split in two. The hard numbers from suppliers (TSMC, Samsung) are excellent, while the funding story behind the biggest buyers is under question. That favours owning suppliers and power over leveraged spenders, which is how the book is built, but chips can sell off on a headline even with strong results. Rate-sensitive power names (NEE, CEG) are exposed to a higher 10-year.

Had the portfolio existed at Thursday's close, it would have gained about 1.0% on Friday against 0.6% for SPY. Constellation (+4.6%) and Vistra (+3.4%) did most of the work and TSMC (-1.0%) was the drag. This is a pro-forma figure, not a track record.
